# State notes for cleaning businesses

These are orientation notes, not legal advice. Figures change every year: **always look up the current number for the specific state and city before quoting it** and write the date next to it. Anything marked (verify) must be confirmed with the state agency, a CPA or an employment attorney.

## General (all states)
- Federal: minimum wage $7.25 but almost every metro we serve is far above it; overtime after 40h/week (FLSA); I-9 for every hire; new-hire reporting to the state.
- Worker classification: cleaners on set schedules with company supplies = employees. 1099 is high risk everywhere and near-impossible in CA (ABC test), MA, NJ, IL.
- Workers' comp: required once you have employees in nearly every state (thresholds vary; verify).
- Business registration: LLC in home state; foreign qualification when you have employees/locations in another state (verify nexus rules).
- Multi-state payroll: withhold for the state where the employee works; register for withholding and unemployment in each such state.

## Texas
- No state income tax. Franchise (margin) tax applies above a revenue threshold (verify).
- **Sales tax: residential and commercial cleaning/janitorial services are taxable in Texas.** Get a sales tax permit and charge state + local rate by job location (verify current rates).
- Workers' comp is optional in TX ("non-subscriber") but non-subscribers lose common-law defenses; strongly recommend carrying it.
- Minimum wage follows federal; market pay is higher. No state-mandated meal/rest breaks for adults. Final paycheck: 6 days after termination (involuntary) / next regular payday (resignation) (verify).
- Hurricane season (Gulf coast) and extreme heat: OSHA heat guidance, water/rest for crews.
- Cities like Austin/Houston/Dallas: no special cleaning license; check local business registration.

## Florida
- No state income tax. Annual report for LLC due May 1 with fee (verify).
- **Sales tax: nonresidential (commercial) cleaning services are taxable in Florida; residential cleaning generally is not** (verify current rule and local surtax).
- Minimum wage is indexed and rising each Sept 30 toward $15 (verify current figure).
- Workers' comp: required at 4+ employees for non-construction (verify).
- Counties/cities require a local business tax receipt (occupational license).
- Hurricane season June–Nov: cancellation policy for storms, post-storm cleanup demand; snowbird season (Nov–Apr) boosts residential and STR turnover demand.

## California
- **Classification: ABC test (AB5).** Treat cleaners as W-2 employees. Independent-contractor cleaners are a major liability.
- High labor cost: state minimum wage plus many city minimums (LA, SF, San Jose, etc.), daily overtime after 8h, meal and rest breaks with premium pay if missed, paid sick leave, final pay immediately on termination (verify all).
- Sales tax: services (including cleaning) generally not taxable; tangible goods sold are.
- LLC annual franchise tax ($800) plus LLC fee by revenue (verify).
- Workers' comp mandatory from the first employee.
- Contracts: automatic renewal law requires clear disclosure and easy cancellation; non-competes are void; strong consumer protection.
- Wildfire smoke: smoke-cleanup demand and worker protection rules (Cal/OSHA).
- Some cities require business registration/tax certificate (e.g., LA, SF).

## Northern states (typical: MA, NY, NJ, CT, PA, IL, MN, WI, MI)
- Generally high minimum wages and strong employee protections; several have mandatory paid sick leave and paid family leave (MA, NY, NJ, CT, IL, MN) (verify each).
- Classification tests are strict in MA, NJ, IL (ABC-style). Use W-2.
- Sales tax on cleaning: varies — e.g., NY taxes many maintenance/cleaning services, NJ taxes some; MA and IL generally do not tax residential cleaning (verify per state).
- Workers' comp mandatory from the first employee in most (verify).
- Winter: snow/ice cancellations, salt-stain floor care demand in commercial, spring-cleaning peak March–May, move season concentrated May–Sept (NYC/Boston Sept 1).
- Cities (NYC, Chicago, Boston) may have additional wage rules, fair workweek/scheduling ordinances (verify).
